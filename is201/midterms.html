Midterm Group Report: Workplace Computer Ethics Case Analysis

The Requirement

Overview

Each group will identify and critically analyze a real-world computer ethics issue experienced or directly observed by at least one group member in their workplace or organization.

The purpose of this requirement is to apply the concepts, ethical frameworks, and principles discussed in IS 201 to an authentic situation and develop a reasoned and ethically defensible response.

25%

Of your final course grade

The Midterm Group Report is due on November 15, 2026, 11:59:59 PM (GMT+8), submitted through MyPortal as a compiled PDF and a ZIP of the complete LaTeX project.

The Requirement

Case Selection

Each group must identify one real-world issue involving computing, information systems, data, digital technologies, or the professional practice of computing.

The case must have been experienced or directly observed by at least one group member in their workplace or organization. It should not be based solely on a news article, hypothetical scenario, or case found online.

The case must involve a meaningful ethical tension, dilemma, or conflict. A purely technical or operational problem is not sufficient.

An event is not yet an ethical problem. “The organization experienced a data breach” describes an event but does not by itself constitute an ethical problem. An ethical case might instead examine whether and how the breach should be disclosed, who should be informed, how competing organizational and stakeholder interests should be balanced, and what responsibilities computing professionals have when faced with conflicting obligations.
The Requirement

Confidentiality and Disclosure

The actual organization does not need to be named in the report. Groups may use a pseudonym or a general description of the organization. Names of individuals, systems, projects, clients, and other identifying information should likewise be anonymized when appropriate.

Do not disclose confidential, proprietary, personally identifiable, security-sensitive, or otherwise restricted information.
Disclose privately to the professor
  • The actual name of the organization
  • The group member who experienced or directly observed the case

This information is for verification purposes only and should not appear in the report.

Exercise professional judgment when presenting workplace situations. The objective is to analyze the ethical problem, not to expose or investigate particular organizations or individuals.

Report Structure

The Report at a Glance

Use the shared LaTeX template, which already contains the title page, abstract, table of contents, every section heading, and the tables described below. Your report should move from understanding the case to defending an ethically reasoned decision, in this order:

Abstract→ I. Background→ II. Ethical Issues→ III. Stakeholders→ IV. Ethical, Professional & Societal Analysis→ V. Evaluation & Recommendations→ VI. Summary

Followed by References (APA 7th edition) and three Appendices: Individual Ethical Reflections, Group Contribution Statement, and Declaration of AI Use.

The template contains guide text and notes in each section. Delete all guide text before submission.
Report Structure

Abstract

Give an overall view of the report in not more than 250 words. Briefly state:

  • the workplace context of the case, without identifying the organization;
  • the central ethical problem and the major stakeholders;
  • the ethical frameworks and principles applied;
  • the course of action the group recommends, and why; and
  • the main lesson of the case for ethical responsibility in computing or information systems practice.

Write the abstract last, after the rest of the report is complete.

Report Structure · Section I

Case Background and Context

Establish the factual and contextual foundation of the case.

  • Describe what happened or what is happening.
  • Explain the technological, social, institutional, organizational, and legal context necessary to understand the case.
  • Support the account with credible evidence or sources, such as academic, professional, legal, institutional, or industry sources.
  • Explain why the case matters from a computer ethics perspective.

Provide enough information for a reader unfamiliar with the organization to understand the situation without unnecessarily revealing confidential or identifying information.

Describe the organization generally. Use a pseudonym or a general description, such as “a private financial services company,” “a government agency,” or “a higher education institution,” and anonymize the names of employees, clients, systems, and projects.
Report Structure · Section II

Problem and Ethical Issues

Clearly identify the problem being examined.

Central Ethical Problem — State the central ethical problem or dilemma that the group seeks to resolve, framed as a genuine question about what ought to be done.
Specific Ethical Questions — Identify the specific ethical questions arising from the case, such as questions of rights, duties, harms and benefits, fairness, autonomy and consent, privacy, transparency, accountability, or competing obligations, and connect each to the facts of the case.
Dimensions of the Problem — Distinguish among the technical, legal, organizational, professional, and ethical dimensions of the problem.
Make clear why the issue cannot be resolved solely by determining what is technically possible, legally permissible, or organizationally convenient.
Report Structure · Section III

Stakeholder Analysis

Identify and analyze the major stakeholders involved in or affected by the case, by role rather than by name. For each significant stakeholder or stakeholder group, consider its:

Interests Rights Responsibilities Vulnerabilities Power or influence Benefits and harms Conflicting interests

Demonstrate how different decisions may affect different stakeholders, and how competing interests or responsibilities contribute to the ethical complexity of the case.

Table 1 in the template: Summary of Stakeholder Analysis
  • Use it to organize the analysis, adding or removing rows as needed.
  • It does not replace substantive discussion. Follow the table with a discussion of what it reveals.
Report Structure · Section IV

Ethical, Professional, and Societal Analysis

This section forms the core of the report.

Analyze the case through the ethical frameworks discussed in the course, the professional, legal, and organizational obligations that apply, the wider societal implications of the case, and any additional ethical principles the case requires. The ethical frameworks discussed in IS 201 should serve as the primary analytical lenses.

For each applicable framework or additional principle

  1. Explain what it considers ethically or morally relevant.
  2. Explain why it is applicable to the case.
  3. Apply it systematically to the facts and circumstances of the case.
  4. Determine what action, responsibility, or position it would support.
  5. Identify limitations, tensions, or conflicts in its application.
Simply defining or summarizing ethical frameworks, principles, codes, or laws is not sufficient. The emphasis should be on applying them to reason through the actual ethical problem.

A. Ethical Frameworks Discussed in the Course

Apply all ethical frameworks discussed in the course that the group determines to be applicable to the case, following the five steps above. Present each framework under its own heading.

B. Professional, Legal, and Organizational Obligations

Examine what the profession, the law, and the organization require in this case, and who is responsible for meeting those requirements. Assess whether these obligations were met and whether they were adequate to the situation. Consider:

  • responsibilities of computing and IT professionals;
  • relevant professional codes or standards, such as the ACM Code of Ethics and Professional Conduct;
  • applicable laws and regulations, such as the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (RA 10173), the Cybercrime Prevention Act of 2012 (RA 10175), or the Intellectual Property Code of the Philippines (RA 8293); and
  • organizational policies or governance mechanisms.
Compliant is not the same as ethical. Distinguish among legal, organizational, professional, and ethical obligations in the case. Something may comply with a law or organizational policy and still raise significant ethical concerns.

C. Societal Implications

Examine the case beyond the immediate ethical dilemma. Discuss its wider effects on users, institutions, society, and the computing profession, such as effects on public trust, digital inclusion, or professional norms.

D. Additional Ethical Principles

Identify and justify additional ethical principles that address what Subsections A to C leave unresolved, drawing from appropriate scholarly literature, professional codes, standards, or established ethical guidelines, and cite the sources.

  • Explain the gap each principle addresses.
  • Apply each principle following the same five steps, and present each under its own heading.
  • Do not repeat obligations already examined in Subsection B.

E. Synthesis

Conclude the section by synthesizing the analysis. Identify where the frameworks, obligations, societal considerations, and principles converge, where they lead to different conclusions, and what ethical tensions or trade-offs remain unresolved. This synthesis provides the basis for evaluating the courses of action in Section V.

Report Structure · Section V

Ethical Evaluation and Recommendations

Develop at least three plausible and realistic courses of action in response to the ethical problem.

The alternatives should represent meaningfully different responses rather than minor variations of the same solution.

A–C. Courses of Action 1 to 3

Present each course of action under its own subsection, with a short description in its heading. Additional courses of action may be added as further subsections. For each one:

  • explain what the proposed response entails;
  • analyze its potential benefits and advantages;
  • analyze its risks and potential harms;
  • consider its implications for the relevant stakeholders;
  • examine its ethical consequences and trade-offs; and
  • evaluate it using the ethical analysis developed earlier.

D. Comparison of the Courses of Action

Compare the courses of action with one another. Table 2 in the template (Comparative Evaluation of Courses of Action) may be used to summarize the comparison, but it should be accompanied by analytical discussion.

E. Recommended Course of Action

  1. Identify the course of action the group considers most ethically defensible, and provide a clear and reasoned argument for this position.
  2. Explain why the selected course of action is preferable to the alternatives.
  3. Connect the decision to the ethical frameworks, principles, and evidence presented earlier.
  4. Provide concrete and realistic recommendations to the relevant stakeholders, such as management, IT staff, policy owners, and affected users.

F. Counterargument and Response

Identify the strongest reasonable counterargument to the group's position, stated fairly and in its most persuasive form, and respond to it.

The objective is not merely to state what the group believes. The group must defend its position through ethical reasoning.
Report Structure · Section VI

Summary

Provide a concise summary of the case and the group's analysis. The section should:

  • restate the central ethical problem;
  • summarize the most important insights from the stakeholder, ethical, professional, and societal analyses;
  • state the group's final position and key recommendations; and
  • highlight the main lesson or implication of the case for ethical responsibility in computing or information systems practice.

The Summary should synthesize the report rather than introduce new arguments, evidence, or issues.

Report Structure

References

Use credible and appropriate sources to support factual, theoretical, legal, professional, and contextual claims. Use APA Style (7th edition) for both in-text citations and the reference list.

In-text citations — Use the author–date format, e.g., (Santos, 2024) or Santos (2024). Include page numbers for direct quotations, e.g., (Santos, 2024, p. 15).
Reference list — List entries alphabetically by the first author's surname, with a hanging indent, following the APA format for each source type. In the template, type each entry as its own paragraph inside the apareferences environment; the hanging indent is applied automatically.

Every source cited in the text should appear in the References, and every entry in the References should be cited in the text. Verify that each source exists and supports the claim for which it is cited, including sources suggested or generated by AI tools.

Report Structure

Appendices

Appendix A. Individual Ethical Reflections

Each group member must provide a short, individually identified reflection, under a heading with their name, addressing:

  1. What aspect of the case did you find most ethically difficult or significant?
  2. Did your initial position change after the group's analysis? Why or why not?
  3. What responsibility does a computing or information systems professional have when encountering a similar situation?

The reflection should demonstrate the member's own ethical engagement with the case and should not simply summarize the group report.

Appendix B. Group Contribution Statement

Identify the substantive contribution of each group member in Table 3 of the template. The statement should make clear how each member participated in the investigation, analysis, writing, and/or revision of the report.

The final submission should read as one coherent report, not as independently written sections simply combined into one document. All members remain collectively responsible for the quality and integrity of the final submission.

Appendix C. Declaration of AI Use

Declare whether and how AI tools were used in completing the requirement, including generative AI tools and AI-powered writing feedback such as Studiosity. If AI was used, identify:

  • the AI tool(s) used;
  • the purposes for which each tool was used;
  • the parts or stages of the work in which AI assistance was used; and
  • how the group reviewed, verified, revised, or otherwise took responsibility for the AI-assisted output.

Record each use in Table 4 of the template, one row per tool and purpose. If no AI tool was used, delete the table and replace it with an explicit statement to that effect.

The declaration does not exempt the group from responsibility for the submitted work. All members remain responsible for its accuracy, originality, ethical reasoning, citations, and overall integrity.

Performance Levels

Excellent — Demonstrates comprehensive, insightful, and well-supported analysis. Ethical reasoning is rigorous and nuanced, and conclusions follow convincingly from the evidence and analysis.
Proficient — Demonstrates sound and generally well-supported analysis, with only minor gaps in depth, integration, or reasoning.
Developing — Demonstrates partial understanding or analysis. Important aspects may be insufficiently developed, overly descriptive, weakly supported, or inadequately integrated.
Insufficient — Demonstrates limited ethical analysis or substantial misunderstanding of the case. Major requirements are missing, inadequately addressed, or based largely on unsupported description or opinion.
Closing

Important Reminders

Authenticity of the case — The case must originate from the actual workplace or organizational experience of at least one group member. Invented cases or cases taken entirely from published sources do not satisfy this requirement.
Focus on ethical analysis — A detailed description of what happened is not, by itself, an ethical analysis. The report should progress from understanding the case to identifying the ethical issues, analyzing stakeholders, applying ethical frameworks and principles, examining professional and societal dimensions, evaluating alternatives, and defending an ethically reasoned decision.
Confidentiality and professional responsibility — Do not include information that could unnecessarily compromise the privacy, confidentiality, security, or legitimate interests of individuals or organizations. Anonymize, generalize, or omit information where appropriate.
Group responsibility — This requirement is designed for a group of 4–5 students and should demonstrate meaningful intellectual participation from all members.
AI use — AI tools may support the group's work where permitted, but it must not substitute for the group's own investigation, ethical reasoning, judgment, or individual reflection. All use of AI must be declared in Appendix C. The group remains fully responsible for the submitted work.
Closing

Guiding Question

Given the actual circumstances of this workplace case, what is the most ethically defensible course of action, and why?

Every section of your report should help answer this question.
